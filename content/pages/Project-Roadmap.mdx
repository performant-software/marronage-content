      #### PHASE I: OUR FIRST PROTOTYPE

      The initial phase of this project aimed to build a prototype and a set of standard methodologies, vocabularies, and tools to identify, document, and georeference the cases we were studying. As we imagined the possibilities of our endeavors, we aimed to create scalable resources that could bring together different Caribbean regions often studied apart as separate units of analysis. We also envisioned creating digital maps that reconstructed the geographic trajectories of cimarronaje and thus challenged the geographical orders usually presented in colonial official maps–which hide and omit the presence of Black, indigenous, and mixed-race communities. The prototype phase aimed to test the possibilities of trans-regional study, and we used samples from Jamaica and Venezuela to begin this step. 

      During this phase, we used different types of software to create our deliverables. For the first iteration of these datasets, we used tabular CSV tables. However, as our needs changed, we migrated our data to a different program, Nocodb, an open-source database software. The final step of phase one involved ingesting these relational databases into ArcGIS, the software of choice for the prototype. 

      #### PHASE II: DATA MIGRATION AND WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT

      As we wrapped up Phase I, we encountered the challenge of making our resources sustainable over time. While ArcGIS was instrumental in crafting the mapping design for our project, we needed to migrate our data to an Open-Access infrastructure for flexibility, sustainability, and customization.  Thanks to the Caribbean Digital Scholarship Collective seed grant, we worked with Performant Software to launch the second phase of the Cimarrón Geographies Project. Performant Studio offers a standards-based software platform for digital humanities projects that integrates many of our needs, from relational databases to mapping and website hosting. We continued performing website updates and data curation. 

      #### PHASE III: DISSEMINATION

      We have participated in several Digital Humanities convenings to show our early results. Our first process essay will be published in Archipelagos Journal in 2027.  

      #### PHASE VI: SCALATION AND STORYTELLING

      We continue expanding the project by curating the data, updating basemaps, and setting standards that enable us to integrate other partners and collaborators. We also plan to turn our attention to storytelling to show the people, movement, and histories behind the data in our project.
