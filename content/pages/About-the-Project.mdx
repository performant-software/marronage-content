      imarrón Geographies creates a comprehensive digital atlas of maroon communities in the Spanish Caribbean between 1520 and 1804. The project addresses a critical gap in Caribbean historiography: while scholars recognize marronage as central to colonial history, we lack systematic spatial documentation of where these communities formed, how they moved, and how they shaped colonial territorial expansion. Using archival materials, the project maps settlement locations, commercial routes, relationships with colonial and ecclesiastical institutions, and patterns of migration and displacement. The resulting database and interactive maps allow users to ask cross-regional comparative questions about maroon geographies in the Caribbean, revealing that these communities were not peripheral to the colonial order but were constitutive of its territorial and institutional formation and limits.
